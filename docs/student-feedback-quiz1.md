# Student feedback for the Quiz 1 tutor

Purpose: find factual mistakes and usability problems quickly, and learn whether the tool supports recall. Feedback is voluntary; this is not part of the student's course assessment. Do not require names, student IDs, full chat histories or official exam responses.

## A short pilot (about 8–10 minutes)

1. Try two image-identification cards and one map-labelling card before revealing answers.
2. Ask one short Lecture 1 question.
3. Ask one comparison across lectures or regions, e.g. Uruk and Mohenjo-daro.
4. Open a citation and check whether the highlighted passage supports the answer.
5. Scroll to another page, then revisit one card marked Again.

## Required feedback for a useful report

1. **What did you try?** Feature, lecture/material, exact question, and source page/slide if available.
2. **Was anything factually wrong or misleading?** Name/place/date/period, interpretation presented as fact, or an unsupported comparison. Include the problematic sentence and what the course source says instead.
3. **Did the citation help you verify the claim?** Correct page? Correct highlighted text? Relevant evidence? If not, identify the mismatch.
4. **Could you complete the task?** Describe any broken image, unreadable extract, confusing control, page jump or generation failure. Device/browser and an optional screenshot help reproduction.

These four items are more useful than a star rating alone.

## Short ratings (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree)

- I could find the material I needed.
- The answer was short and clear enough for Quiz 1 revision.
- Image and map questions were readable and unambiguous.
- I tried to recall the answer before revealing it.
- Revisiting missed cards helped me notice what I did not know.
- The source links helped me decide whether to trust an answer.
- The waiting time was acceptable.

Follow each low rating with “What should change?” Ask whether the student used phone, tablet or computer, and whether a reply was immediate, about 3–10 seconds, or longer than 10 seconds. These are reported experiences, not measured timings.

## Quiz-format fit and confidence

- Which practice types felt closest to the announced Quiz 1 format?
- Which topic or question type was missing, too easy, too broad or outside Lectures 1–3?
- Did a front image, caption or source label accidentally reveal the answer?
- Did your confidence change after checking the original source? Were you ever confident in an answer that proved wrong?
- What is the single most important change before the quiz?

## Optional learning check

With the professor's agreement, use a few matched, unassisted practice questions before and after tool use (different questions of similar difficulty). Record aggregate changes and uncertainty; self-reported confidence or a satisfaction score alone does not establish improved learning. Do not use the real Quiz 1 paper as an unapproved research instrument.

## How to prioritize findings

**Fix first:** incorrect identity/date/place, source mismatch, answer-revealing cards, unsupported certainty, broken core controls or inability to load materials.

**Fix next:** slow replies, unclear wording, oversized questions, confusing navigation or insufficient coverage.

Report counts by feature and lecture, typical waiting experience, examples of critical errors and which fixes were retested. Review accuracy complaints against the actual course source with the professor; do not automatically treat student preferences as a marking rubric.
